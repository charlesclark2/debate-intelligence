The Whitefish Bay High School debate team competes in three events: Policy debate,
Lincoln-Douglas debate and Public Forum debate. Students research a question, prepare the case
for both sides of it, and argue it in front of a judge. What each event looks like is explained on
[what the events are](/events/).

The program has been running for several decades. More than 20 students are competing this
season.

## Where we compete

The team belongs to three organizations, and competes under all three.

- The **Wisconsin Debate Coaches Association (WDCA)**, which runs the Wisconsin high school
  circuit and the state tournament.
- The **National Speech and Debate Association (NSDA)**, the national organization whose district
  tournament is one route to the national tournament.
- The **National Catholic Forensic League (NCFL)**, whose league qualifies students to its own
  national tournament.

Students choose which tournaments they attend. A typical Wisconsin tournament means arriving at
the host high school around 8:00 AM and competing into the afternoon. To qualify for the state
tournament, a student needs to attend at least two tournaments and post a record of three wins and
two losses.

If there is enough interest this season, the team hopes to add a couple of overnight trips to
Chicago, Minneapolis or Iowa to compete on the national circuit, which debaters call the
Tournament of Champions circuit.

## How the season runs

The season starts in early October and runs through Martin Luther King Jr. weekend in January.
Students pursuing national qualification keep going into the summer, with a much lighter travel
schedule in the second semester.

Practice is twice a week at lunch and once a week after school; the times are on
[how to join](/join/).
